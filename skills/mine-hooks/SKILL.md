# MINE Hooks

## Purpose
Turn a topic, niche, or draft into 10 scroll-stopping hooks built on patterns mined from real performing content — not generic hook formulas.

## When to use
- User asks for hooks, openers, video intros, or first lines.
- User has a topic but doesn't know how to start the piece.
- Any MINE content pipeline step that needs the opening 3 seconds.

## The mined patterns
These come from teardown analysis of real outperforming posts. Reach for them before inventing:

1. **Proof hook** — lead with a real number. Strongest documented: a real dollar figure in the first 3 seconds ("$500 last night — 2-hour Rolls Royce rental").
2. **Transaction hook** — filmed real handoffs/transactions outperform staged content (top teardown format: 270K likes on a real handoff).
3. **Name-it hook** — name the specific thing (the car, the place, the person). Small accounts: naming the subject drove 58% engagement rate at 799 followers.
4. **Enemy hook** — name what you're against. The Five Enemies frame: Fear, Self-Doubt, Hate, Being Comfortable, Stagnation.
5. **Ore hook** — "we mined this" — position the content as extracted value: "108M views taught me this."
6. **Contrarian hook** — flip the expected advice in the niche.
7. **Story-gap hook** — open mid-story with the outcome withheld.
8. **Identity hook** — speak to who the viewer is becoming, not what they lack.
9. **Question hook** — a question the viewer is already asking themselves.
10. **Direct-call hook** — call out the exact viewer ("If you're a creator posting daily and nobody's watching...").

## Rules
- One idea per hook. Never two.
- Under 12 words unless the number needs room.
- No clickbait the content can't pay off — the hook is a promise, the content is the proof.
- Write in the user's voice, not guru voice. Plain-spoken.
- Every hook must survive the first-3-seconds test: would a scroller stop?

## Workflow
1. Ask for (or infer): niche, topic, and who it's for.
2. Pick the 3–4 patterns with the best fit for this topic.
3. Write 10 hooks, each labeled with its pattern.
4. Star the top 3 and say why in one line each.
5. Offer to expand the winner into a full script (hook → proof → method → CTA).
